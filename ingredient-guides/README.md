# Ingredient Guide

A quick reference for commonly used cooking ingredients — how to use them, how to store them, and one handy tip for each.

| Ingredient | Common Uses | Storage Advice | Tip |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Garlic** | Sautéing, marinades, sauces, roasting | Store whole bulbs in a cool, dark, dry place with airflow; do not refrigerate | Crush cloves with the flat of a knife before mincing to release more flavor and make peeling easier |
| **Onion** | Base for soups/stews, sautéing, garnishes, pickling | Keep whole onions in a cool, dry, well-ventilated spot away from potatoes; refrigerate once cut | Chill onions for 10–15 minutes before cutting to reduce eye irritation |
| **Olive Oil** | Sautéing, dressings, drizzling, finishing dishes | Store in a cool, dark cupboard away from heat and light; keep tightly sealed | Use a robust extra-virgin oil for dressings and a lighter one for high-heat cooking |
| **Eggs** | Baking, binding, sauces (mayo, custard), frying/scrambling | Refrigerate in original carton, away from strong-smelling foods | Bring eggs to room temperature before baking for better volume and emulsification |
| **Rice** | Side dishes, stir-fries, risottos, pilafs | Store uncooked rice in an airtight container in a cool, dry place | Rinse rice before cooking to remove excess starch for fluffier grains |
| **Butter** | Baking, sautéing, sauces, spreading | Refrigerate wrapped or in a sealed container; freeze for longer storage | Use unsalted butter for baking so you can control the salt in the recipe |
| **Salt** | Seasoning, curing, brining, baking | Store in a dry, airtight container at room temperature | Season food in stages while cooking rather than all at once for better-balanced flavor |

## Notes

- Storage advice assumes typical home kitchen conditions; always check packaging for specific guidance.
- This guide covers general-purpose staples; specialty or perishable ingredients may need different handling.
